## Identity

Red Bull DNA: vertical, transition-heavy football with aggressive counter-pressing and young athletic profiles. Ole Werner (2025/26, sacked June 2026 despite 3rd place and 65 points) rebuilt after losing key players and had no European football to manage. Demichelis, appointed within five days of Werner's exit, brings a more possession-oriented positional game from his River Plate title-winning side; he opened 2026/27 with a 4-3-3 (Vandevoordt; Baku, Orbán, Lukeba, Raum; Banzuzi, Seiwald, Reitz; Nusa, Gomis, Gruda) and a 3-0 win over Gladbach.

Principles: under-24 recruitment with resale value, red bull multi-club network (salzburg, new york, bragantino), coach must fit the system, not vice versa, sell one star per summer

Character: Corporate-backed club founded 2009; small but growing fan base; low fan pressure but high boardroom expectations; polarising in German football culture because of the 50+1 workaround.

## Strengths

- Third in the Bundesliga with 65 points under Werner despite a full rebuild; 3-0 opening win under Demichelis.
- Financial firepower from the Diomandé sale (EUR 125m+) and Red Bull backing.
- Baumgartner (13 league / 17 total goals) provides goals from midfield; Raum was the squad's top-rated player in 2025/26.
- Deep young talent pipeline via the Red Bull network; Nkunku returned on loan as an experienced option.

## Weaknesses

- Coaching discontinuity: third head coach in three seasons; Demichelis has no prior Bundesliga or Champions League experience.
- Loss of Diomandé removes the main wide creative outlet; Gruda/Bakayoko/Nusa must replace 1v1 output.
- Striker output unproven: Openda sold to Juventus, Gomis scored 1 in 21 games last season, Harder and Rômulo are young, Nkunku is a loan.
- No European football in 2025/26, so the squad has no recent midweek/weekend rhythm; Gulácsi's departure leaves a 24-year-old No. 1 with a veteran free-agent backup.
