## Identity

Fàbregas plays a Guardiola-school 4-2-3-1: patient possession, a double pivot (Da Cunha/Perrone) that builds through the thirds, creative No.10s (Nico Paz, Baturina) and full-backs who invert or overlap. The 2026 window doubled down on this by adding ball-playing defenders (Chalobah, Yan Couto, Cuenca, Kaiki) and technical midfielders (Ricci, Milla).

Principles: young high-ceiling talents bought outright (nico paz eur 60m from real madrid), coach-led project with long-term contract, heavy owner investment ahead of revenue, international, multilingual squad

Character: Owned since 2019 by Indonesia's Hartono family (Djarum group) via SENT Entertainment; small city, small stadium (Sinigaglia) but among the wealthiest ownerships in Italy; low media pressure compared with Milan/Turin clubs.

## Strengths

- Clear, coach-owned tactical identity (positional 4-2-3-1) and the coach staying despite Chelsea/Barcelona interest.
- Elite creative core for a Pot-4 club: Nico Paz (bought for EUR 60m), Baturina, Diao.
- Defence rebuilt with Champions-League-experienced players (Chalobah, Yan Couto, Sánchez).
- Owner financial power allows net spending far above Pot-4 peers; no need to sell.
- Deep Coppa Italia run (semi-final) shows cup-tie competence.

## Weaknesses

- First-ever European campaign: zero UCL experience as a club, small stadium (Sinigaglia) and infrastructure.
- Squad churn of ~23 moves in one window - integration risk for a possession system that needs automatisms.
- Douvikas/Kean pairing unproven at UCL level; Morata's experience gone.
- Full-back depth relies on new arrivals (Couto, Kaiki) after Vojvoda, Van der Brempt, Cassandro and Moreno all left.
- Final 2025/26 points/GF/GA could not be sourced, so the underlying performance level is unquantified here.
