# Kesire Yıldırım (Öcalan), May 1978 – end 1982: whereabouts vs. the Pekmezci "SSK records" claim

Reliability grades: A = primary/official or contemporaneous document; B = participant memoir or serious journalism with named sources; C = mainstream press retelling / Wikipedia-type secondary; D = anonymous, partisan, or single-source allegation.

## 0. The claim under test

Necdet Pekmezci, *PKK'yı Kim Kurdu? Derin Devletin Gizemli Adamı Pilot Necati* (Kaynak-type "derin devlet" series; also *Apo, Pilot ve Derin Devlet*): SSK records allegedly show Kesire Yıldırım insured at private employers in Ankara in 1979 (workplace "Ali Rıza S.") and 1981 (workplace "Bünyamin D."), and that she held an Emekli Sandığı (civil-servant pension) number as a teacher.

Search status: no online source (news, blog, sözlük, Aydınlık columns summarising Pekmezci, book-shop blurbs) reproduces the "Ali Rıza S." / "Bünyamin D." / SSK detail. Searches for `Pekmezci Kesire SSK`, `"Kesire" "Bünyamin" Öcalan`, `Kesire Emekli Sandığı`, `Kesire sigortalı Ankara 1979` returned nothing on-point. The book blurbs only say it "questions whether Öcalan's ex-wife Kesire was an intelligence officer" (kitapyurdu / D&R / mardinlife listings). The SSK claim therefore stands, as far as the open web shows, **solely on Pekmezci's text**, and I could not confirm the exact wording of the book. Treat the exact claim as UNVERIFIED (grade D until the page itself is read).

## 2. Assessment of the SSK / Emekli Sandığı claim

**Emekli Sandığı number as a teacher — plausible and unremarkable.** She taught at a state primary school in Karakoçan (1970–73/74). Any state teacher, even a *vekil* (substitute) on a paid posting, would have been enrolled in T.C. Emekli Sandığı. This part of the claim, if true, proves nothing about 1979–82 and does not require any posting in Çınar.

**SSK insurance at Ankara private workplaces in 1979 and 1981 — contradicted by the best available chronology, but not impossible in 1979.**

- 1981: the strongest sources (PKK's own Serxwebûn history; Nedim Şener's Swedish-sourced column; the Marcus/IKHRW line; Nesra) put her in **Sweden from spring 1981 to January 1984**, running PKK's Europe work. A 1981 SSK entry at an Ankara private employer ("Bünyamin D.") is therefore **contradicted** unless (a) the entry is for a few weeks in Jan–Mar 1981 before she left, (b) the record belongs to a namesake, or (c) an employer registered her name fraudulently (a common practice in Turkey for padding insured days — "sahte sigortalılık").
- 1979: the public record is thin. Öcalan left Turkey June/July 1979; all sources say Kesire followed "in 1979" without a month. Nothing rules out her being in Ankara for part of 1979 (she was an Ankara resident until mid-1978, had family/party contacts there, and was not — as far as any source shows — wanted by police before 12 Sept 1980). So a 1979 Ankara SSK entry ("Ali Rıza S.") is **untestable/possible**; it would simply mean she stayed in Turkey longer than the "went to Syria in 1979" line implies, or held a legal cover job for a few months.

**Which reading fits if the records are genuine?**
1. *Stayed in Turkey under cover, 1979 and (part of) 1981 in Ankara.* Fits 1979 weakly; for 1981 it collides with the Sweden chronology unless the job predates spring 1981. Also she would have been working under her own name — after the 12 Sept 1980 coup the PKK's Fis-congress members were being rounded up and Öcalan's wife would have been an obvious target; no source says she was ever detained. That makes an own-name 1981 Ankara job implausible unless she was protected (which is exactly Pekmezci's implied thesis — so the claim is circular unless the record itself is produced).
2. *Records belong to a namesake.* "Kesire Yıldırım" is a rare first name but Yıldırım is extremely common; SSK ledgers of that era were matched by name + father's name + birth date. Without those fields the match is unproven.
3. *Fictitious insurance (sahte sigortalılık).* Employers/relatives registering absent people was widespread; her father Ali Yıldırım was an Ankara resident (MİT/CHP milieu). This would explain records without physical presence.
4. *Pekmezci misread or invented.* His broader "derin devlet" framing is polemical; the book has no footnoted archive references that any reviewer cites.

**Verdict:** The teacher/Emekli Sandığı part is plausible but trivial. The 1981 Ankara SSK entry is *contradicted* by independent sources placing her in Sweden from spring 1981. The 1979 entry is *untestable* on open sources. The claim as a whole is unverified and should not be repeated as fact.

## 4. "Devrim nikâhı" vs. Altındağ Evlendirme Dairesi — is it settled?

- No source at all uses "devrim nikâhı" for this marriage. Sources say "yıldırım nikâhı" (a quick civil wedding).
- No source mentions **Altındağ** Evlendirme Dairesi. The consistent, registry-backed version (Sabah 26 Dec 2012, Yeni Şafak, Aydınlık) is **Ankara Belediyesi Gençlik Parkı Nikâh Salonu, 24 May 1978, witness Muzaffer Ayata**, and the nüfus kaydı shows the marriage still in force. (Gençlik Parkı lies in Altındağ district, so the two are not necessarily in conflict — the salon was the municipality's central wedding hall.)
- Verdict: the civil marriage is settled (grade B, registry-based). The "devrim nikâhı" label is unsupported.

## 6. What would be needed to verify

1. The actual pages of Pekmezci's book: does he reproduce a document (SSK hizmet dökümü / sicil no.), and with which identifiers (baba adı Ali, doğum 21.10.1951 Karakoçan)?
2. SGK 4/a service record under her T.C. identity — obtainable only by her, her heirs, a court, or a prosecutor; not public. A court file (e.g. the 1999 Ankara DGM Öcalan case or any Diyarbakır Sıkıyönetim file naming her) might include a "sosyal güvenlik sorgusu".
3. Emekli Sandığı (now SGK 4/c) teacher sicil: MEB personnel record for Yeniköy İlkokulu, Karakoçan 1970–74 — would settle the teacher-number point and show whether any later posting (Çınar?) existed.
4. Swedish Migrationsverket/Statens invandrarverk file 1981–84 (residence permit for Kesire Öcalan/Yıldırım) — would nail down the 1981 departure and destroy or rescue the 1981 Ankara entry.
5. Serxwebûn Jan 1982 issue 1 (scribd.com/doc/123656044) and the 1st Conference documents: check delegate/assignment lists for "Fatma".
6. Full text of Serxwebûn's "44 yaşında" retrospective for the exact sentence on Fatma being in Europe (grade B+ as quoted; needs exact wording).
7. Diyarbakır 1981 PKK main indictment (Sıkıyönetim 2 No.lu Askeri Mahkeme) to see if she was an in-absentia defendant — establishes whether working under her own name in 1981 was even conceivable.

## 7. Bottom line

- Teacher/Emekli Sandığı number: plausible, proves nothing.
- 1979 Ankara SSK entry: untestable; not contradicted, but no source places her in Ankara after mid-1978 either.
- 1981 Ankara SSK entry: contradicted by three independent lines (PKK's own Serxwebûn history, Swedish-side reporting via Şener, Marcus-type account) placing her in Sweden from spring 1981 to Jan 1984.
- She was not at the July 1981 conference (in Europe) and very probably not at the Aug 1982 congress.
- No one besides Pekmezci is known to have cited her social-security records; Mumcu and the 2011–12 Sabah/Yeni Şafak pieces used MİT-personnel and nüfus records, not SSK.
- "Devrim nikâhı"/Altındağ framing is unsupported; the registry-backed Gençlik Parkı civil wedding of 24 May 1978 is settled.
- Residence now: recent Turkish press says Netherlands (2025); older sources Sweden; one says Switzerland — unverifiable.
